    Why the south pole is different

    • The Moon barely tilts, about 1.5°. At the pole the Sun never climbs: it circles the horizon once every 29.5 days.
    • Because the Sun is so low, a ridge a few hundred metres high can hide it for days, and a crater floor can stay dark for billions of years. Press Play on the map to watch the shadows swing round.
    • Earth does not rise and set the way the Sun does. It hovers in one part of the sky and bobs up and down about every 27 days, so a lander loses its direct radio link whenever Earth dips behind the skyline.
    • Try this: compare the ridge next to Shackleton crater with the crater floor, 13 km away. Then compare both with Apollo 11 near the equator.

    Same sunlight, three ways to mount a solar panel

    Full-sun hours count how long the panel would need to face the Sun head-on to collect the same light. A flat panel wastes low sunlight; an upright one catches it.

    A body counts as visible when its centre is above the skyline. Earth must also clear the minimum margin. This is geometric visibility: it does not model antennas, ground stations or battery size. Full-sun hours measure head-on sunlight time for the chosen panel, not energy.
